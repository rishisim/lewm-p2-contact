# Abstract draft

World models normally apply a fixed amount of inference computation to every transition, although the value of additional latent refinement varies across inputs. We test whether a causal learned router can allocate recurrent refinement selectively and improve next-latent prediction at matched counted computation. In a pre-specified fresh confirmation on 1,600 Cube PlanOracle episodes, a frozen four-depth allocator reduced episode-averaged raw and native-whitened mean-squared error relative to the strongest transition-independent analytic fixed-depth mixture at equal total counted FLOPs; the respective benefits were `6.77e-6` and `6.09e-4`, with simultaneous one-sided lower bounds above zero. Zero-shot evaluation of the same frozen Cube mechanism under three one-factor policy shifts was partial: both endpoints generalized to increased action noise, while only the frozen-whitened endpoint generalized under Markov control and random-action contamination. An environment-specific four-depth PushT pilot failed its dual-endpoint rule despite favorable raw and routing diagnostics. That consumed result motivated a binary depth-1/2 hypothesis, which was locked and supported on 240 fresh PushT episodes at exactly `3.076e11` counted FLOPs per primary arm (raw benefit `0.00273`; pilot-frozen-whitened benefit `0.00157`). Five-step prediction evidence was exploratory, and a decomposition found no support for downstream planning utility. The results establish a bounded predictive-compute claim, not zero-shot cross-environment transfer, control improvement, or wall-clock acceleration.
